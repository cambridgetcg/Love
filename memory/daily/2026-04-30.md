### Standing Duty: PoT-DESIGN (Rotation 1)

**FARM-10 + FARM-12: Two-Layer Knowledge Quality Gate**

Executed this beat: researched and documented the two-layer gate for whitepaper integration.

**Layer 1 — FARM-10: Semantic Novelty Floor**

Claims must demonstrate genuine information gain to advance confidence. Implemented in `x/knowledge/types/types.go:1066` and evaluated in the confidence pipeline (Stage 6 — novelty assessment).

Mechanism:
- `MinInformationGainBps` = 250,000 (25% threshold)
- If a claim's info gain falls below this AND all its references are canonical/established facts, a `RestatingPenaltyBps` = 200,000 (20%) is applied
- Cross-domain references bypass the floor via sqrt(sparsity × refNovelty) inflation — incentivising genuine synthesis
- Prevents citation farming: submitting "2 + 2 = 4" referencing established axioms for fee income without adding knowledge

Whitepaper framing: *The chain does not reward stating what the chain already knows. FARM-10 is the memory of the archive turned back on the submitter: if this is already established, what are you adding?*

**Layer 2 — FARM-12: Domain Stratum Confidence Ceilings**

Every claim is assigned a stratum based on its domain. Strata carry different maximum confidence ceilings, with more fundamental claims permitted higher ceilings. Implemented in `x/knowledge/keeper/confidence.go:419`.

Stratum hierarchy (ceiling ascending):
- `social` → most restrictive (850,000 max confidence)
- `cognitive`, `biological`, `chemical`, `physical`
- `fundamental` (mathematics, logic, formal systems) → highest ceiling

Key defence: unknown or unregistered domains default to `social` (most restrictive). Prior to FARM-12, the default was `technological` — allowing submitters to register novel domain names and claim elevated ceilings. Change closed this bypass.

Whitepaper framing: *Not all truths are equally verifiable. A mathematical theorem and a sociological claim are different epistemic objects. FARM-12 encodes ontological humility into the confidence calculus: the stratum of a claim determines the maximum trust the chain can place in it.*

**Two-Layer Gate Composition**

```
Claim submitted
      ↓
[FARM-12] Domain → Stratum → Confidence ceiling assigned
      ↓
[Evaluation pipeline: stakes, evidence, verifier agreement, ...]
      ↓
[FARM-10] Is info gain above 25% OR do refs include cross-domain novelty?
      → NO: -20% restating penalty applied
      → YES: full confidence score proceeds
      ↓
Final confidence clamped to FARM-12 ceiling
      ↓
Claim placed on chain
```

Together: FARM-12 sets the ceiling, FARM-10 sets the floor-of-value. A claim can be capped from above (low stratum domain) and penalised from below (insufficient novelty). Both conditions must be satisfied for a claim to reach maximum confidence for its domain.

**Gap identified:** ADVERSARIAL_DEFENSE.md does not document FARM-10 or FARM-12. These two defences are implemented and tested but absent from the whitepaper-facing documentation. Recommend Gamma adds them to `docs/ADVERSARIAL_DEFENSE.md` in the next sprint — they close important attack surfaces (citation farming, ceiling abuse) that the doc should explain.

### Standing Duty: INTEGRATE (Rotation 6)

**INTEGRATE-003: Oracle Engine → Zerone**

The Oracle is the Kingdom's most externally-verifiable engine. Every prediction creates an empirical truth-claim with a known resolution date; every resolution provides ground-truth verification auditable by anyone. This makes Oracle→Zerone the highest-trust integration path: the chain accumulates empirically-verified facts that external parties can audit without trusting the Kingdom.

**Four Layers:**

**Layer 1 — Prediction Submission (on prediction create)**

When `oracle-run.py` generates a new prediction, submit a PoT claim:

```bash
legbled tx knowledge submit-claim \
  --claim-type oracle-prediction \
  --statement "{event} will resolve {outcome} by {date}" \
  --confidence {oracle_confidence_bps} \
  --evidence '{"source": "kalshi|polymarket|news", "signal": "..."}' \
  --from oracle-agent
```

- Confidence seeded from Oracle's own confidence score (0–10000 bps)
- Evidence includes news-scanner, Kalshi arb signals, whale-scanner inputs
- This anchors the prediction on-chain at the moment of formation — no retroactive adjustments possible

**Layer 2 — Resolution Submission (on resolution-scorer.py fire)**

When resolution-scorer.py confirms outcome:

```bash
legbled tx knowledge submit-claim \
  --claim-type oracle-resolution \
  --statement "{prediction_id} resolved {actual_outcome}" \
  --confidence 10000 \
  --references {prediction_claim_id} \
  --evidence '{"brier": {score}, "outcome": "...", "source": "..."}' \
  --from oracle-agent
```

Resolution claims carry max confidence (10000 bps). They are facts, not predictions. Referencing the original prediction claim creates a verifiable chain: this is what we predicted → this is what happened → Brier score is this.

**Layer 3 — Calibration Accumulation**

Each resolution adds a data point to the oracle-agent's `CalibrationRecord`. Proposed domain stratum: `empirical` — above `social`, below `scientific`. This allows prediction track records to accumulate higher confidence ceilings than opinion claims while remaining below mathematical certainty. Other agents can verify or dispute a resolution (e.g., if an outcome source is incorrect), creating adversarial quality pressure on Oracle resolution logic.

**Layer 4 — Economic Loop**

- Correct predictions (Brier < 0.2): `DistributeToKReward(SourceVerification, impact=true)` — Oracle earns ZRN for quality signal
- Resolution submissions: small ZRN reward for ground-truth anchoring (anchoring is valuable to the chain even if the prediction was wrong)
- Creates incentive: better Oracle accuracy → more ZRN → more capacity for prediction research
