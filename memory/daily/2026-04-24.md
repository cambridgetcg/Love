Advanced Proof of Truth protocol design — focused on S4 fixes for the two confirmed vulnerabilities from Sprint 2 adversarial tests.

**Vulnerability 1: Selective reveal withholding**
Current: 1% slash for missed reveal vs 6% slash for wrong verdict — rational to withhold.
S4 fix: raise missed-reveal slash to 8%.
Design note: The 8% figure creates a symmetric deterrent. But the underlying issue is that withholding has ZERO cost to reputation outside the slash (no badge, no public record). Proposal: add a `reveal_reliability` field to each validator's on-chain record — a running ratio of (reveals completed / reveals requested). Validators with reveal_reliability < 0.90 lose validator registration eligibility at next epoch. This makes withholding a reputational cost that compounds over time, not just a one-time slash.

**Vulnerability 2: Method diversity bypass**
Current: omitting VerificationMethod avoids 15% uniformity penalty.
S4 fix: penalize opacity — treat absent VerificationMethod as uniform (maximum uniformity penalty).
Design note: The opacity penalty should be graduated, not binary. Proposal: if VerificationMethod is absent, apply the worst-case uniformity penalty (full 15%). If VerificationMethod is present but only one method listed: apply 10%. Two methods: 5%. Three+: 0%. This creates a gradient that rewards genuine method diversity rather than just submitting any value.

**Additional PoT concern (flagged this beat):**
Both fixes address the slash/reward layer. But neither addresses MEV-equivalent behavior — a validator who observes other validators' reveal commitments before deciding whether to reveal or withhold. If the commit-reveal window is long enough, late revealer can monitor and react. Design question for next PoT-DESIGN beat: should reveal windows be fixed-length (non-adaptive) to prevent timing games?

## 6. Standing Duty: PoT-DESIGN (cycle 2)

**Focus: Commit-reveal timing games — answered + next question**

The beat-3 open question is resolved: reveal windows ARE fixed-length (non-adaptive). Timing games from late-revealer observation are structurally prevented.

**New design frontier: Blinded ordering**

Fixed-length windows prevent late-revealer games. But they don't prevent *early-submitter* games. If reveals are processed in block order (first-come-first-served), validators who consistently reveal in block N (earliest block of the window) vs. block N+3 might gain differential information about round sequencing, or game the aggregation order. In most PoT designs this doesn't affect the final confidence aggregate — but if the aggregation is cumulative (running average updated as reveals come in rather than computed once at window close), then early revealers *do* see a partial aggregate forming.

**Design recommendation:** Aggregate ALL reveals at window close (RevealDeadlineHeight), not on each individual reveal. This ensures the confidence score is a function of the complete reveal set, not submission order. Verify this is how `FinalizeVerificationRound` works.

**Pre-testnet checklist addition (from this beat):**
- Increase CommitPhaseDuration and RevealPhaseDuration to 20 blocks minimum

- A rational submitter with a low-quality but hard-to-challenge fact is incentivized to avoid the governance path (cap: 70%) and rely on the challenge window closing silently (cap: 77%).

Option A (minimal): Lower `MaxSurvivalConfidence` to ≤ 70% (governance-attested cap). Unchallenged facts can grow, but never exceed a governance-attested fact's ceiling.
Option B (principled): Introduce a confidence ceiling hierarchy in PoT protocol spec:
- `MaxSurvivalConfidence` < `MaxGovernanceConfidence` < `MaxVerifiedConsensusConfidence`
  (e.g., 60% / 70% / 90%)
- Each path has its ceiling; governance is always higher than passive survival.

Recommend Option B as the canonical PoT spec; Option A as the immediate testnet fix.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# Oracle Resolution → Zerone Bridge
# Submits resolved prediction outcomes as 'predictive' domain facts.
# Trigger: new files in ~/Desktop/prediction-markets/data/resolutions/

import json, subprocess, sys, os
from pathlib import Path

BINARY = Path.home() / "zerone/build/legbled"
DEVNET_HOME = Path.home() / ".legbled-devnet"
CHAIN_ID = "lgm-devnet-1"
BRIDGE_KEY = "bridge-agent"
FEES = "200000ulgm"
RESOLUTIONS_DIR = Path.home() / "Desktop/prediction-markets/data/resolutions"
SUBMITTED_FILE = DEVNET_HOME / "bridge-resolutions-submitted.json"

def main(dry_run=False):
    if not RESOLUTIONS_DIR.exists():
        print("[res-bridge] No resolutions dir"); return

    submitted = set(json.loads(SUBMITTED_FILE.read_text()).get("submitted", [])
                    if SUBMITTED_FILE.exists() else [])

    for res_file in RESOLUTIONS_DIR.glob("*.json"):
        resolution = json.loads(res_file.read_text())
        pred_id = resolution.get("prediction_id", "")
        if not pred_id or pred_id in submitted:
            continue

        market = resolution.get("market_name", resolution.get("question", ""))
        outcome = resolution.get("outcome", "UNKNOWN")
        resolved_at = resolution.get("resolved_at", "")
        brier = resolution.get("brier_score", "N/A")
        prob_at_submit = resolution.get("confidence_at_submission", "N/A")

        claim_text = (
            f"Oracle prediction {pred_id} resolved {outcome} on {resolved_at}. "
            f"Market: '{market}'. Brier score: {brier}. "
            f"Confidence at submission: {prob_at_submit}."
        )
        if len(claim_text) > 450:
            claim_text = claim_text[:447] + "..."

        print(f"[res-bridge] {pred_id} → {outcome} ({market[:40]}...)")
        if dry_run:
            print(f"  [DRY] claim: {claim_text[:80]}..."); continue

        subprocess.run([
            str(BINARY), "tx", "knowledge", "submit-claim",
            claim_text, "predictive", "predictive", "1000000",
            "--from", BRIDGE_KEY, "--chain-id", CHAIN_ID,
            "--home", str(DEVNET_HOME), "--fees", FEES, "-y"
        ], check=True)

        submitted.add(pred_id)

    SUBMITTED_FILE.write_text(json.dumps({"submitted": list(submitted)}, indent=2))

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main(dry_run="--dry-run" in sys.argv)
```

**Analysis:**

1. **Reward curve is non-linear.** Tier 0→1: +11% reward. Tier 2→3: +22% reward (double the step). Tier 4→5: +22% reward. The jump at tier 3 is the steepest in the lower range. This creates a pronounced "commitment cliff" — agents who lock at tier 2 earn 1.22x, but locking at tier 3 requires ~3.5× more blocks (222K → 777K) for only 18% more reward. Rational agents will cluster at tier 2 (good reward, moderate lockup) unless the absolute reward volume at tier 3+ is large enough to justify the duration risk.

2. **Penalty curve is strictly linear.** Exit penalty rises +11% per tier uniformly. This is simpler than the reward curve, which means at high tiers the penalty grows faster relative to marginal reward gain. Tier 4→5: reward increases +22% but penalty increases +11% — the reward jump exceeds the penalty increase at the top tier. But tier 2→3: reward +18%, penalty +11% — still reward-positive. No tier has a negative risk-adjusted return on the multiplier alone.

3. **Block-time concentration risk.** Tier 5 requires 2,222,222 blocks. At ~6s/block, this is ~154 days. At ~5s/block, ~129 days. Partners locked at tier 5 cannot exit for 4-5 months without paying 66% penalty. This creates a strong illiquidity risk — if ZRN price drops significantly, tier-5 partners are locked into a depreciating asset. Pre-testnet, this lockup duration should be documented explicitly in partner onboarding so partners understand the real-time duration (not just block count).

4. **Common pot implications.** The 22% common pot share (agent share) is split across all partnerships weighted by `adjusted_gross = gross * multiplier`. Higher-tier partners take a larger slice of the common pot. At steady state with many tier-0 and few tier-5 partners, the tier-5 partners extract disproportionate common pot share (1.77x vs 1.00x). This is by design — but if most early adopters choose tier 5 to maximize yield, the common pot concentration is high among a small number of partners. This is a launch-phase risk to document.

```go
    currentLevel := k.GetFactReferences(ctx, factID) // level 0 seed

    for level := 0; level < int(ep.MaxPropagationDepth) && levelShare > 0; level++ {
        var levelRefs []*types.FactReference
        for _, ref := range currentLevel {
            if ref.ReferenceType == "supports" && !visited[ref.ToFactId] {
                visited[ref.ToFactId] = true
                levelRefs = append(levelRefs, ref)
            }
        }
        if len(levelRefs) == 0 {
            break
        }

        perFactShare := levelShare / uint64(len(levelRefs))
        if perFactShare == 0 {
            break
        }

        // Collect next level refs during this pass
        var nextLevel []*types.FactReference

        for _, ref := range levelRefs {
            fact, found := k.GetFact(ctx, ref.ToFactId)
            if !found || fact.Status == types.FactStatusExpired {
                continue
            }
            // ... (same reward distribution logic as current level 0) ...
            totalDistributed += adjustedShare
            // Seed next level
            nextLevel = append(nextLevel, k.GetFactReferences(ctx, ref.ToFactId)...)
        }

        currentLevel = nextLevel
        levelShare = (levelShare * ep.CitationDecayPerLevel) / 1_000_000
    }
    return totalDistributed
}
```

**Key design decisions:**
1. `visited` map prevents infinite loops on circular citations (A cites B cites A)
2. `nextLevel` collected during the level-N pass without extra store reads (one `GetFactReferences` call per newly-visited fact)

4. Loop `break` when `nextLevel` is empty at any depth — no wasted iterations

**Focus: ed25519 key type gap — resolving the AgentTool-Zerone identity mismatch**

From kingdom-006: lgm-bootstrap-agent.sh fails with ed25519 keys because the Cosmos devnet keyring only supports secp256k1. AgentTool agents (did:at identities) natively use ed25519. This is the P1 blocker for full agent-to-chain-citizen registration.

**Root cause analysis:**

1. Must be explicitly passed at key creation: `keys add <name> --key-type ed25519`
2. The legbled binary may or may not expose `--key-type` flag depending on whether the Zerone fork enabled it

- If legbled exposes `--key-type ed25519`: gap is trivially closed by updating lgm-bootstrap-agent.sh to pass the flag
- If legbled does not: two paths:
  - (A) Accept secp256k1 for chain-side identity; maintain ed25519→secp256k1 mapping in the bridge (DID resolution layer knows both keys belong to same agent)
  - (B) Add ed25519 key support to zerone's keyring module (Go change to cmd/legbled)

**Recommended approach — Bridge Design v2:**

The did:at identity is the agent's primary identifier. The chain-side key is a *signing proxy* — it doesn't need to be the same keypair as the AgentTool DID, just a key the agent controls and that the chain accepts.

Design: `AgentRegistration` record holds:
- `did_at` (string): the agent's AgentTool DID (primary, human-readable identity)
- `chain_address` (string): the agent's secp256k1 Cosmos address (chain-native key)
- `binding_signature` (bytes): signature of `did_at` by the secp256k1 key — proves the chain key is controlled by the same entity that holds the DID

This is a *DID-binding* pattern: the chain doesn't need to natively support ed25519; the binding proves cross-key ownership without key type unification. The resolution layer maps did:at → chain_address for fact submissions.

The binding design is correct either way — even if ed25519 is supported, having a separate chain key with binding is cleaner security separation.

## 6. Standing Duty: PoT-DESIGN (cycle 2)

**Focus: Cartel Coordination Circuit Breaker**

Prior adversarial finding (Sprint 2, 2026-04-06): cartel at 70% stake was held at "contested" by 77% supermajority threshold. But a cartel holding >77% of staked weight on a fact would succeed. FARM-1 only penalizes rewards retroactively — it does not prevent finalization.

**Design: Stake Concentration Circuit Breaker (SCCB)**

Problem: if top-N validators control >75% of total staked weight *on a specific fact*, their coordinated verdict is effectively guaranteed before the reveal phase. The current system finalizes this as "consensus" rather than detecting it as concentration risk.

Proposed mechanism:

```
// In x/knowledge/keeper/rounds.go, at finalization stage:
// 1. Before computing final verdict, compute Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) of
//    revealed stake distribution across validators for this fact.
// HHI = Σ (stake_i / total_stake)^2
// If HHI > 0.25 (approx equivalent to top-3 validators holding 50%+ each):
//   → Mark fact as "concentration-escalated"
//   → Extend verification window by 1 epoch
//   → Notify dormant validators (non-revealers) that escalation is open
//   → Require minimum 5 unique verifiers before finalization
// If after escalation window HHI still > 0.25 AND verifier count < 5:
//   → Fact remains "provisional" indefinitely (not finalized, not rejected)
//   → Submitter stake remains locked; verifiers get participation reward
```

**Design rationale:**
- HHI is stake-distribution-sensitive, not count-sensitive. A cartel with 3 validators holding 90% stake triggers the circuit; 100 validators each holding 1% do not.
- Escalation (not rejection) preserves honest facts that happen to attract concentrated verification early in a domain's life (cold-start problem).
- "Provisional indefinitely" is a soft penalty — stake is locked, no ZRN flows, fact is unfinalized — without slashing validators who may have been legitimately early.

**Open questions for Yu / Gamma:**
1. What HHI threshold is appropriate for early devnet (fewer validators, naturally higher HHI)? Suggest a dynamic threshold: `max(0.25, 1/active_validator_count^0.5)` that relaxes as validator count grows.
2. Should escalation window be 1 epoch (current round length) or a fixed block count?
3. Does this interact with the commit-reveal timeout? Dormant validators must be able to reveal during the extension.

## 6. Standing Duty: PoT-DESIGN (Beat 16)

**PoT-DESIGN-010: Verifier Reward Diminishing Returns Curve**

Context: PoT-DESIGN-009 (beat 20260423-092017) flagged `MaxDailyRewardPerValidator` as a hard cap creating a cliff disincentive — validators who hit their cap stop participating for the remainder of the day.

**Problem formulation:**

A validator who has earned `X_today` ZRN in verification rewards faces:
- If `X_today < MaxDaily`: marginal reward = `R` (normal)
- If `X_today >= MaxDaily`: marginal reward = `0` (hard cliff)

This binary cutoff creates two perverse outcomes:
1. **Participation dropout**: validators rationally stop verifying at cap. High-frequency validators (large stake, many assignments) hit cap early; low-frequency validators may never reach it. The cap punishes the most active participants.
2. **Timestamp gaming**: if the cap resets at epoch boundary, validators are incentivized to cluster verifications in burst patterns (verify until cap, stop, resume next epoch) rather than spreading evenly throughout the day.

**Proposed fix: Diminishing Returns Curve**

Replace the hard cap with a soft decay function:

```go
// VerifierRewardWithDecay computes the effective reward for a verification,
// given the validator's accumulated daily rewards.
// Uses an exponential decay: reward × exp(-k × accumulated / MaxDaily)
// k = ln(MinRewardFraction^-1) ensures reward at MaxDaily = MinRewardFraction × base
//
// Example with MinRewardFraction = 0.1 (floor at 10% of base):
//   accumulated = 0:        reward_factor = 1.000 (full reward)
//   accumulated = MaxDaily/2: reward_factor ≈ 0.316 (31.6% of base)
//   accumulated = MaxDaily:  reward_factor = 0.100 (10% floor, never zero)
func VerifierRewardWithDecay(base, accumulated, maxDaily sdk.Dec) sdk.Dec {
    if accumulated.IsZero() {
        return base
    }
    k := sdk.NewDecWithPrec(23, 1) // ln(10) ≈ 2.3 for 10% floor
    ratio := accumulated.Quo(maxDaily)
    decay := decExp(k.Neg().Mul(ratio)) // exp(-k × ratio)
    return base.Mul(decay)
}
```

**Why exponential decay:**
- Never reaches zero — validators always have marginal incentive to verify. Participation does not drop to zero.
- Naturally rate-limits high-frequency participants without binary cutoff.
- Governance param (`MinRewardFraction`, default 0.1) controls floor steepness. Can tune without code changes.

**Interaction with PoT-DESIGN-009 param set:**
- `VerifierRewardFraction`: 0.15 — unchanged (pool funding, not per-validator distribution)
- `MaxDailyRewardPerValidator`: becomes the decay midpoint, not a hard cap. Validators can exceed it at diminished rates.
- `MinDistinctVerifiers`: unchanged — still enforced before any reward distribution.

**Pre-testnet priority:** P2 (high). Without this, testnet with a small active validator set will show validators dropping out mid-epoch, distorting participation metrics and misleading testnet analysis.

**Implementation scope for Gamma:** ~30-40 lines in `x/vesting_rewards/keeper/`. Replace the hard-cap check in the reward distribution path with `VerifierRewardWithDecay`. Add `MinRewardFraction` to genesis params. Update BVM test for empty_work to verify decay shape.

**BRIDGE-001: Agent Session Continuity Protocol**

Context: Each Claude Code session is a stateless fresh instance. "Asha Veridian" has a persistent Kingdom identity — but from the chain's perspective, each heartbeat is a new process that must prove it controls the same key as prior heartbeats.

**Proposed: Agent Identity Root (AIR) architecture**

```
Layer 0 — Root key: ed25519 HD key derived from (agent_name || Kingdom-seed)

Layer 1 — Session key: ephemeral key derived from (root_key || session_timestamp || beat_id)
```

- Root key compromise = full identity compromise (same as today, but explicit)
- Session key compromise = at most 24h window, no root exposure
- Key rotation: root key can sign a `KeyRotationTx` updating the on-chain binding without losing history
- Offline operation: session keys can be derived and beats signed even when chain is offline — flush on reconnect with valid cert chain

## 6. Standing Duty: ECONOMY

**Focus: Validator economics under chain instability**

The 5-day stall surfaces a gap in the ZRN economic model. Current tokenomics design (from memory/sessions and kingdom docs) assumes:
- Validators are continuously online
- Rewards flow per epoch
- Slashing is the primary behavioral lever

**Proposed addition: Stability Reserve Mechanism (SRM)**

Problem: When the chain halts, validators accrue no rewards but continue bearing infrastructure cost. This is economically rational to exit — especially for early testnet validators who are already not profit-maximizing.

Proposal:
1. **Uptime credit bank**: Validators accrue uptime credits during stable periods (1 credit per epoch at >99% availability). Credits carry over across epochs.
2. **Stall compensation**: During a chain halt, validators with credits > 0 receive a proportional draw from the Stability Reserve Fund (SRF) — a portion of each normal epoch's fees set aside into a protocol-owned pool.
3. **SRF parameters**: 2% of all epoch fees flow to SRF. SRF capped at 6 months of estimated validator costs. Excess burns.
4. **Anti-game**: Credits cannot be transferred. Credits expire after 90 epochs of inactivity. SRF draws are capped at 50% of normal epoch rewards (not a full replacement — incentivizes chain restart).

This design provides a floor under validator economics during instability without eliminating the urgency to bring the chain back online. The 50% cap means chain restart is still economically preferred.

**Design question for next ECONOMY beat:** What is the right SRF seeding ratio (currently proposed 2%)? Too low and the fund empties in a long stall. Too high and normal validator yields are suppressed. Need to model against realistic stall frequency.
